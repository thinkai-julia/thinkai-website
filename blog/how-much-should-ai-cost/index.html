How Much Should AI Actually Cost a Small Service Business?

August 22, 2026 · 1 min read · By Julia, Founder of ThinkAI

This question comes up early in almost every conversation about AI, and it's a reasonable one to ask. It's also one that doesn't have a single honest answer, because it depends entirely on what's actually being built.

A strategy engagement, a single AI phone line, and a full multi-system automation buildout are different projects with different costs — pricing follows scope, not the other way around.

Why a flat number is a red flag

Anyone quoting a fixed price before understanding a business's specific workflows, integrations, and goals is pricing based on a template, not the actual work. That template price rarely matches what actually gets delivered.

What actually drives the cost

The number of systems that need to connect, the complexity of the workflow being automated, and how much strategy or creative work is involved all move the price. A single AI phone agent answering FAQs costs very differently than a system integrating a CRM, scheduling, and multi-channel follow-up.

Start narrower than "everything"

Trying to price and build an entire connected system at once is both expensive and risky. A phased approach — start with the highest-priority bottleneck, prove it works, then expand — is usually more practical and easier to budget for honestly.

The real question to ask instead

Rather than "how much does AI cost," a more useful question is: "what's the cost of the problem this would solve?" A slow follow-up process or a missed-call problem has a real cost already, even if it's never been calculated. That's the number worth comparing against.
